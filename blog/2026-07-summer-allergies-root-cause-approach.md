# Summer Sniffles: A Root-Cause Approach to Seasonal Allergies

For a lot of Bradford families, summer comes with an unwelcome companion: the sneezing, itchy eyes, and stuffy mornings of seasonal allergies. Grass pollen in early summer, ragweed as the season turns — the triggers shift, but the misery feels familiar.

At WellnessRX, we like to ask a different question than "what can I take to mask this?" We ask, "*why* is the body reacting this strongly, and how can we calm the whole picture?" That's the heart of a holistic, root-cause approach to health and wellness — and with allergies, it opens up more options than you might expect.

## What's really happening with allergies

An allergic reaction is your immune system overreacting to something harmless, like pollen. The goal isn't to "shut off" your immune system — it's to help it stay calm and balanced so it stops sounding the alarm over a blade of grass. That balance is influenced by more than just the pollen count: your gut health, your stress levels, your sleep, and your overall inflammation all play a part.

## A whole-person way to ease the season

**Reduce your exposure where you can.**
- Keep windows closed on high-pollen days and use air conditioning
- Shower and change clothes after time outdoors to rinse pollen away
- Check daily pollen forecasts and plan outdoor time for lower-count hours

**Support a calmer immune response from the inside.**
- A gut-friendly, whole-foods diet supports balanced immune function — much of your immune system lives in your gut
- Colourful fruits and vegetables provide natural compounds that help moderate the body's inflammatory response
- Staying well hydrated keeps the mucous membranes in your nose and sinuses working as they should

**Don't underestimate sleep and stress.** A tired, stressed body tends to react more, not less. Protecting your rest and building in calm is genuinely part of allergy care.
