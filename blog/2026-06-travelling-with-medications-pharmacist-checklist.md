# Heading Away This Summer? A Pharmacist's Checklist for Travelling with Medications & Supplements

Summer is travel season — cottage weekends, road trips, and flights to see family. As your pharmacist, I want those trips to be about rest and connection, not a scramble over a forgotten prescription or a supplement that doesn't agree with your travel plans.

A little planning goes a long way. Here's the clinical-but-holistic checklist we walk through with patients before they head out.

## 1. Count your days — then add a buffer

Before you leave, do a quick count: how many days will you be away, and do you have enough of each medication to cover them? Then add a few extra days in case of travel delays. If you're going to run short, this is the moment to arrange a refill — not the night before you leave.

If your trip is long enough that a refill will come due while you're away, talk to us early. We can often help you plan ahead so you're never caught without an essential medication.

## 2. Keep medications in their original containers

It's tempting to consolidate everything into one small pill organizer, and that's fine for daily use — but for travel, especially across a border, keep prescription medications in their original, labelled containers. The label connects the medication to you and your prescriber, which matters at security and in any medical situation away from home.

## 3. Pack a "just in case" kit

Pack medications in your **carry-on**, not checked luggage, so a delayed or lost bag never means a missed dose. A simple travel kit might also include:
- A small first-aid basics supply
- Anything you reach for occasionally (allergy relief, motion sickness support, pain relief)
- A written list of your medications, doses, and any allergies

## 4. Mind the heat

Summer heat isn't kind to medications. A glovebox, a hot car, or a sunny windowsill can degrade certain products — and some, like insulin, need careful temperature control. Keep medications out of direct heat, and ask us if anything you take needs special storage on the road.

## 5. Don't forget your supplements — and how they travel

Supplements deserve the same thought as prescriptions. A few things to keep in mind:
- **Timing may shift** across time zones — we can help you map out when to take what.
- **Some supplements interact** with medications, or with how your body handles sun and heat. This is exactly the kind of drug–supplement interaction check we do every day.
- **More isn't "travel insurance."** Loading up on new supplements right before a trip can backfire. If you want immune or digestive support for travel, let's choose something that fits *you*.

## 6. Build your "before you go" habit

Our favourite tip: book a quick medication review before any big trip — or before the busy season starts. Bringing together your prescriptions, your supplements, and your real-life plans in one conversation is the heart of integrated, patient-centered care. It's a few minutes that can save a lot of stress.

## Your pre-trip checklist
- [ ] Enough of every medication, plus a buffer
- [ ] Refills arranged before you leave
- [ ] Medications in original labelled containers
- [ ] Everything essential in your carry-on
- [ ] Heat-sensitive items stored safely
- [ ] Supplement timing and interactions reviewed
- [ ] A written medication + allergy list packed
