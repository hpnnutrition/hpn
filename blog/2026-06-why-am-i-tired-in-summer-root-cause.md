# Why Am I So Tired in Summer? A Root-Cause Look at Low Energy

Summer is supposed to be the season of energy — long days, sunshine, time outdoors. So why do so many people tell us they feel *more* tired, not less? If you've been dragging through these warm Bradford afternoons, you're not imagining it, and you're not alone.

At WellnessRX, we don't believe fatigue is something you just push through. We believe in a holistic, root-cause approach to health and wellness — asking *why* the tiredness is there in the first place. Often it's not one big thing, but several small, very fixable things stacking up.

## The common root causes of summer fatigue

**1. Quiet dehydration.** Heat and sweat pull water *and* minerals out of your body faster than you might notice. Even mild dehydration shows up as low energy, brain fog, and headaches long before you feel thirsty.

**2. Mineral and electrolyte loss.** Sweating heavily without replacing sodium, potassium, and magnesium can leave you flat — even if you're drinking plenty of water. The fix isn't always *more* water; it's better *balance*.

**3. Disrupted sleep.** Hot nights, late sunsets, and a busier social calendar all chip away at sleep quality. And poor sleep is one of the most common — and most overlooked — drivers of daytime fatigue.

**4. Blood sugar roller-coasters.** Summer eating can drift toward quick, sugary, on-the-go choices. Each spike is followed by a crash, and those crashes feel exactly like fatigue. Pairing carbohydrates with protein, fat, and fibre helps keep energy steady.

**5. Nutrient gaps.** Low iron, low B12, and low vitamin D are common, very treatable causes of ongoing tiredness — and they're easy to miss without looking.

**6. Your medications.** This one is often overlooked. Some common medications can contribute to fatigue, affect how your body handles heat, or interact with the supplements you're taking. That's worth a closer look, not a shrug.

## Small steps you can start today
- Hydrate with minerals, not just water, on hot and active days
- Anchor each meal with protein, healthy fat, and fibre to steady blood sugar
- Protect your sleep — cool, dark room and a consistent wind-down
- Get outside for morning light to support your natural rhythm
- Don't self-diagnose persistent fatigue — let's look for the cause
