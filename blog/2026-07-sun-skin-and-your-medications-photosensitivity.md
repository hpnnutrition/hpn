# Sun, Skin & Your Medications: What Every Patient Should Know About Photosensitivity

Here's something that surprises a lot of patients: the sunburn you got "out of nowhere" this summer might have had a little help — from your medicine cabinet. As your pharmacist, this is one of those quiet, easy-to-miss details I love to flag before it becomes a problem.

It's called **photosensitivity** — when a medication or supplement makes your skin react more strongly to sunlight. The result can be a faster, more severe sunburn, or a rash, even after sun exposure that normally wouldn't bother you.

## What is photosensitivity, exactly?

Certain substances change how your skin responds to ultraviolet (UV) light. There are two general types:
- **A heightened sunburn-like reaction** — your skin burns more quickly and intensely than usual
- **A rash or skin reaction** — sometimes appearing as redness, bumps, or itchy patches in sun-exposed areas

You don't need to memorize the science. What matters is knowing *whether anything you take could be involved* — and that's a quick conversation we can have together.

## Common categories that can increase sun sensitivity

Without singling out specific brands, several **categories** of medications and even some supplements are known to increase sun sensitivity for some people, including:
- Certain antibiotics
- Some medications for blood pressure and heart health
- Certain anti-inflammatory pain relievers
- Some skin treatments (topical and oral)
- A number of herbal products and supplements

This is *not* a reason to stop a medication you need — never stop a prescribed medication on your own. It's simply a reason to ask, "Does anything I take call for extra sun care?" That's exactly the kind of supplement-and-medication safety check we do every day.

## A pharmacist's summer sun-safety checklist

Whether or not you take a photosensitizing medication, these habits serve everyone well — and they matter even more if you do:

- **Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen** and reapply regularly, especially after swimming or sweating
- **Seek shade** during peak UV hours (late morning to mid-afternoon)
- **Cover up** with a hat, sunglasses, and light layers on high-sun days
- **Don't forget easy-to-miss spots** — ears, the back of the neck, the tops of feet
- **Be extra cautious near water and sand**, which reflect and intensify UV
- **Watch your skin** — if you notice an unusual rash or a burn that seems out of proportion to your sun exposure, let us know

## The whole-person angle

Sun safety is a perfect small example of integrated, patient-centered care. Your medications, your supplements, and your summer lifestyle don't live in separate boxes — they interact. Bringing them together in one conversation, under one roof, means we catch the little things before they turn into uncomfortable ones.

Next time you're picking up a prescription or starting a new supplement, just ask: *"Should I be doing anything differently in the sun with this?"* It takes two minutes, and it's the kind of question we're always glad to answer.
